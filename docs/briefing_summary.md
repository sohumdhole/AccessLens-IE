# One-Page Executive Briefing: Widening Participation & Catchment Evidence for Maynooth University (`2019/2020` to `2025/2026`)

**Prepared by:** Sohum Vivek Dhole  
**Audience:** Access Office Leadership, Widening Participation Practitioners, and University Committees  
**Evidence Base:** Higher Education Authority (HEA) Student Records System (`2019/2020` to `2025/2026`, `26` institutions) & Pobal HP Deprivation Index 2022 (`3,417` Electoral Divisions, `5,149,139` population across `26` counties)

---

## 1. Purpose of This Briefing

Ireland's **National Access Plan (NAP) 2022-2028** places evidence-driven monitoring at the centre of widening participation policy, setting national targets to increase the higher education transition rate from disadvantaged areas from **`42%` to `54%`**, raise mature disadvantaged new entrants from **`11%` to `20%`**, raise new entrants with a disability from **`12.4%`** to **`16%`**, and raise Traveller new entrants from **`33`** to **`150`**. This briefing summarises what public HEA and Pobal data show for **Maynooth University (MU)** over the seven academic years from `2019/2020` to `2025/2026`.

---

## 2. Five Key Findings in Plain English

1. **Maynooth University is growing faster than the national average:** Total student enrolments at Maynooth rose from **`13,005`** in `2019/2020` to **`15,945`** in `2025/2026` (**`+22.6%`**, or **`+2,940`** additional students, including **`+2.5%`** growth from **`15,560`** in `2024/2025`). Over the same six-year period, the `7`-university sector grew by **`+17.7%`** (`133,780` to `157,495`) and the overall HEA sector grew by **`+15.1%`** (`246,635` to `283,940`).
2. **Mature student numbers are steady, but their percentage share has fallen because young degree intake grew rapidly:** Maynooth enrolled **`3,140`** mature students (aged `24+`) in `2025/2026`, broadly unchanged from **`3,195`** in `2019/2020` (**`3,250`** in `2024/2025`). However, because students aged `23 and under` grew by **`+30.5%`** (`9,810` to `12,805`), the mature share of the student body shifted from **`24.6%` to `19.7%`** (**`-4.9 pp`**), compared with **`30.7%`** across universities and **`34.8%`** across all HEA institutions. Part-time and flexible learners make up **`10.4%`** (**`1,665`** students) at Maynooth vs **`15.3%`** across universities.
3. **Standalone Foundation and Access programmes are a small share of enrolments at Maynooth; the public data cannot show which routes HEAR, DARE or FET entrants take:** In public HEA statistics, **`78.0%`** of Maynooth students (**`12,430`**) are enrolled in Honours Degrees (up **`+28.7%`** from **`9,660`**). Standalone `Foundation and Access` enrolments are **`20`** at Maynooth (out of **`1,035`** nationally), while broader non-honours undergraduate certificate, diploma, and access pathways account for **`880`** students (**`6.6%`** of MU's **`13,310`** undergraduates). Additionally, **`19.8%`** (**`3,150`** students) study in **Education** (`1,385`, `8.7%`) and **Social Care / Welfare** (`1,765`, `11.1%` plus `535` in Health), well above the **`12.9%`** national sector average.
4. **Maynooth draws more of its students from below-average-affluence counties than Dublin university peers:** Nearly one-third (**`32.4%`**, or **`4,775`** students) of Maynooth's **`14,720`** county-mapped Irish students come from the **`19`** counties with below-average Pobal affluence (`score < 0`), compared with **`29.8%`** at DCU, **`23.8%`** at UCD, and **`20.2%`** at TCD. This largely reflects Maynooth's location near the Midlands counties, not only institutional access activity. Alongside its core commuter catchment of **Dublin (`4,530`)**, **Kildare (`2,830`**, where MU educates **`22.0%`** of all HE students from the county), and **Meath (`1,785`**, **`17.0%`** share), Maynooth is a primary university partner for the Midlands, Border, and South-East: **Longford (`12.3%`**, `245` students), **Westmeath (`12.1%`**, `590`), **Cavan (`11.8%`**, `365`), **Offaly (`11.6%`**, `415`), **Leitrim (`9.1%`**, `145`), **Laois (`8.8%`**, `350`), **Wexford (`8.4%`**, `570`), and **Carlow (`8.0%`**, `240`).
5. **County-level affluence strongly predicts higher education participation nationally:** Across all `26` counties (`238,105` Irish-domiciled students, or **`46.2`** per `1,000` residents), more affluent counties have significantly higher participation rates per `1,000` residents (correlation $r = 0.680$, 95% confidence interval **`0.397` to `0.845`**, $p = 0.0001$).

---

## 3. Important Caveats for Decision-Makers

* **County averages are not individual student backgrounds:** Linking county enrolments to county Pobal scores shows **regional patterns** (ecological analysis), not whether an individual student comes from a disadvantaged neighbourhood. Within **Dublin** (affluent county average **`+2.69`**), **`9.6%`** of residents live in disadvantaged Electoral Divisions. Tracking individual disadvantaged entrants requires internal student Eircode matching against the HEA Deprivation Index Score.
* **Disability and entry-route flags require internal MAP records:** Public HEA downloads do not identify DARE (disability), HEAR (socio-economic), QQI/FET, or Traveller/Roma students who enrol directly into Honours Degrees.
* **Privacy protection:** Published HEA numbers are rounded to the nearest **`5`**, and this briefing suppresses cells below **`10`** as `"<10"` (**`206`** cells across the dataset) in line with GDPR-style disclosure control.

---

## 4. Recommended Priorities for the Access Office (MAP)

1. **Protect Mature & Flexible Pathways (`AMBER` KPIs):** With MU's mature share at **`19.7%`** and flexible/part-time share at **`10.4%`**, expand modular, evening, and QQI/FET-linked pathways so mature participation grows in step with overall university expansion.
2. **Integrate Internal Eircode & Entry-Route Microdata with Public Benchmarks:** Combine this public HEA/Pobal baseline with internal MAP student records (HEAR, DARE, QQI/FET, Foundation progressions, and Small Area Eircode deprivation) to track cohort retention, progression, and completion across the student lifecycle.
